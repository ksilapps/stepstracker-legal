Let’s get you moving.

Quick answers to the most common questions about Steps.

Start here

Check the source.

Your activity details show where the numbers came from. Check that source’s permissions first, then reopen Steps to refresh.

My steps aren’t updating. What should I check?

Check the data source and observation time shown with your activity. For iPhone motion data, carry your phone while walking and allow Motion & Fitness. For Apple Health, check that the Health app has recent steps and that Steps can read them. Return to Steps to refresh. A saved-data label means fresh activity may be unavailable.

How do I connect Apple Health?

Open Steps → Settings → Apple Health to request access. In the Health app, open your profile, find Steps under Apps (or Apps and Services), and allow Steps, Walking + Running Distance, and Active Energy as desired. Steps only reads data. iOS does not tell the app whether read access was denied, so missing data does not always mean zero activity.

How do I allow Motion & Fitness?

Open Steps → Settings → Motion & Fitness. If access was denied, use this option to open iPhone Settings, or review Settings → Privacy & Security → Motion & Fitness and enable access for Steps. Then reopen the app.

Where are my Apple Watch steps?

Steps reads Watch activity through Apple Health, not directly from your Watch. Let the Watch sync with your iPhone, check the Health app’s step total, and then reopen Steps. The hourly view uses iPhone motion data, so it can differ from the daily total that includes Watch activity.

Why do distance or calories look different?

Steps uses measured distance when available and estimates it otherwise. Apple Health Active Energy can include activities beyond walking, while estimated walking energy uses your steps and optional body measurements. Check the source label before comparing numbers.

How do I delete my saved data?

Open Steps → Settings → Data → Reset Local Data. This removes saved activity summaries, preferences, achievements, and setup information from Steps. It does not erase Apple Health or iPhone motion history or revoke permissions. The app can read available activity again when you continue using it.
